The Work

Every piece in this shop started as a rough rock. I find the stone, cut it, grind it, polish it, and if it becomes a necklace, I set it myself. Nothing here is mass-produced and nothing is exactly repeatable — two cuts from the same rock can show two different patterns.

First

The cut

Cutting is the decision. Once the saw goes through, you can’t put it back. I cut to show the best face the stone has, and sometimes the stone disagrees with me.

Second

The polish

Grinding and polishing is the slow part. That’s where the color and the banding come up, and the stone starts looking like what it was hiding.

Last

The setting

When a piece becomes a necklace, I set it myself. When it’s a slab or a tower, it stands on its own. Either way, when it sells, that stone is gone. There isn’t a second one.

No production line. One bench, one saw, one pair of hands — and a stone that only ever comes out this way once.

Cut It or Keep It?

Every so often a rough stone lands on the bench and the call isn’t obvious — a band disappearing around the back, a fracture, a face I’d hate to lose. Would you cut this rock, or leave the damn thing alone?

When one of those comes up, I’ll put it here and on the shop’s Facebook page. You pick a side. Then I show what I actually did and why — even if I overruled everybody, and even if the cut was a dud. You get a vote, not a saw.
